HOW TO PLAY

Ungroup is a four-minute race for four colours. You mine faster in a group, but only one member gets paid at a time, and anyone can walk out with a share. Merge, mine, bank, betray.

1. The goal

Every player has a private shopping list: 18 units of one colour and 6 of each of the other three. Nobody else can see yours. The first player to bank the whole list wins the round. If nobody finishes in four minutes, the player closest to finishing wins.

Your list is shown top right as four counters, one per colour: A blue, B yellow, C orange, D red. Progress is the average of how full the four counters are, so a cheap colour (6 needed) is worth three times as much per unit as your big colour (18 needed).

Banked / needed for each colour. This player is 44 percent of the way.

2. The arena

A round disc. Around the rim sit the home pads, one per player; yours is tagged home and an arrow at the screen edge points to it when it is out of view. Inside sit eight mines, two of each colour: an outer ring and an inner ring. A mine is a big circle filled with cells of its colour; the more cells, the more it holds.

You are a small circle. Your push direction is drawn as a little triangle on your rim, coloured by the colour you have declared you are after (your intent, see below).

Pads on the rim, mines in two rings, you in the middle with a yellow intent arrow.

3. Mining

Touch a mine and units flow into your pool, the coloured cells drawn inside your own circle. A mine drains as you mine it and regrows on its own, faster when its neighbours are full. An empty mine goes dark for a while; move on and come back.

Speed is the whole game: a solo body mines 1 unit every 8 seconds. A pair mines 4 times faster, a trio 9 times, a group of four 16 times. Mining rate grows with the square of the group size, so one partner more than doubles what each member earns.

Cheap colours first. Your three 6-unit colours are worth 1/24 of progress per unit; your 18-unit colour only 1/72. Fill the small ones, then grind the big one.

4. Groups

Press J to open your door: a white ring appears around you. When two bodies touch and every member of both is open, they merge into one bigger circle with one shared pool. Groups hold up to six.

A group moves at the average of its members' push directions, and slower the bigger it gets (a pair at 70 percent, a trio at 58 percent of solo speed). Push the same way and you fly; push against each other and you stall. One member who stops pushing simply rides along.

Nobody in a group can change their declared intent, and you cannot join anyone for 3 seconds after leaving a group, or rejoin the same people for 10 seconds.

Dashed white ring = door open. Each member's slice shows its arrow.

5. Banking and the crown

Nothing counts until it is banked. Solo, touch your own pad and your whole pool is banked to you. In a group, only one member's pad pays out, and it pays out the whole shared pool to that member, with a bonus: 15 percent more per extra member. A pair banks 1.15 times its pool, a trio 1.3 times.

Which member? The one wearing the crown, a small gold notch on their slice. The crown goes to the member who is furthest from finishing among those who have been with everyone else for at least 10 seconds. It is handed out again after every merge, bank and departure, so a group that stays together keeps feeding whoever is behind. Newcomers wait their 10 seconds before they can wear it; that stops people from touching a full group at its pad and stealing the payout.

A group has to physically go to the crowned member's pad, so the others carry them there. Banked amounts are whole units; the fraction stays in the pool.

Gold notch = crown. The whole pool goes to that member at their pad.
If you are ahead, the crown will not come to you for a while. That is the moment to decide: stay and carry, or take your share and go.

6. Leaving

Hold L. Your slice starts pulsing so everyone can see it, and after 2 seconds you are out with a share of the pool. Let go before the time is up and you stay.

The share is not an even split. Each colour is divided by declared intent: the members who announced that colour get three parts each, everyone else one part, and the leaver's parts are what they take. Then 15 percent is forfeited to the group as the price of leaving. You are pushed out the back of the group and cannot join anyone for 3 seconds.

Declare your intent with 1 to 4 while you are solo; it is public, shown as the colour of your arrow, and locked once you are in a group. Lying about it costs you: it changes what you would carry out, not what you need.

7. The brand

Leave with 4 or more units and you are branded: a red arc on your rim, for 20 seconds plus 2 per unit taken, up to a minute. Branded players cannot wear the crown while an unbranded member is available, and careful players will not open their door while you are near. Merging with a branded player passes half the mark on to you.

The brand fades with time, and faster if you bank for other people: every unit that goes to a partner while you are marked shortens it by a second.

Red arc = branded. Others will shut their doors.

8. Collisions

Bump into another body hard and both of you spill some carried units onto the floor as small cells, and both are stunned: no moving, no mining. The faster body is stunned two and a half times longer than its victim, so ramming a loaded group costs the rammer more than the target. Spilled units vanish after 8 seconds; anyone who rolls over them keeps them. Bumps between empty bodies just bounce.

9. The shrink

From two minutes in, the arena shrinks to half its radius by the end. Mines outside the edge die (the outer ring goes at about 3:12) and the pads slide inward with the rim, so the late game is played on the inner four mines with the pads right next to them. Plan your big colour around which inner mine has it.

12. Tips for your first round

  1. Open your door at the start and find a partner: two people mine four times as fast as one.
  2. Mine the colours with 6 in the counter before the one with 18.
  3. Watch who has the crown. If it is you, steer the group toward your pad once the pool is worth ten units or so. If it is not, you are carrying them; decide whether that is still worth it.
  4. A leaver takes about a third of a pair's pool and gets branded above four units. Leave when the pool is fat and your pad is close, not in the open.
  5. Past two minutes, the outer mines die. Be on the inner ring with your big colour when they do.
  6. Someone hurtling at you with an empty pool is a rammer. Step off the mine; a stationary body is the only thing they can spill.
Everything you carry can be lost: to a leaver, to a collision, or to the clock. Bank early, bank often.
